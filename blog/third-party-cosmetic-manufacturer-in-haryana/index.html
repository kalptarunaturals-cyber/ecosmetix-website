Third Party Cosmetic Manufacturer in Haryana: Licences, Plant Visits and Launch Planning

26 November 2026 · By the Ecosmetix R&D team

If your brand is based in Delhi NCR, Punjab, Chandigarh, Himachal or Uttarakhand, choosing a third party cosmetic manufacturer in Haryana puts your production within driving distance instead of a flight away. That matters more than it sounds. The products that succeed are usually the ones a founder has touched, smelled and argued over at the bench, and a plant you can reach in a morning makes those sessions easy to arrange. This guide covers what a nearby factory changes for a young brand, how licensing works when a Haryana plant makes goods under your name, what to inspect when you visit, and how to plan the first months of a launch.

We write it as the R&D team of a plant in Ambala, so we will also say plainly how our own process runs, using only facts we publish on this website.

Why brands look for a third party cosmetic manufacturer in Haryana

Most founders do not start their search with a state in mind. They start with a product, then discover that the hardest part of working with any factory is the loop of feedback: a sample arrives, it is nearly right, a note goes back, and a week disappears. Distance stretches that loop. A plant on the Delhi–Chandigarh corridor shortens it for a large share of north Indian brands, because a day trip can replace several rounds of couriers and calls.

Being close brings four practical gains:

  • Bench sessions: you can sit with the formulator and compare three textures side by side, which is far quicker than describing “slightly lighter” in an email.
  • Batch sign-off in person: you can see the first production lot before it is filled, check colour and fragrance against the approved sample, and approve on the spot.
  • Audits without a travel budget: a quality review of the plant, which investors and marketplaces increasingly ask about, costs you a drive rather than flights and hotels.
  • Simple collection: urgent cartons for a launch event or a photoshoot can be picked up by road instead of waiting on a transporter.

None of this means a brand in Mumbai or Bengaluru cannot work with a north Indian plant; we dispatch across India and much of the work runs remotely. It simply means proximity is a real advantage for brands in the region, and worth weighing alongside formulation strength, testing and price.

How licensing works with a third party cosmetic manufacturer in Haryana

Cosmetics made in India are governed by the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, framed under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act. For manufacturing, the rules make the state the regulator: Rule 4(2) states that “The state drugs controller, by whatever name called, shall be the State Licensing Authority” for the manufacture of cosmetics (Cosmetics Rules, 2020). For a plant in Ambala, that means the Haryana drug control authority, not the central regulator, issues and inspects the manufacturing licence.

A few points from the rules are useful for brand owners to know:

  • A manufacturer applies in Form COS-5 and is granted a licence in Form COS-8. A brand that wants its own loan licence to use someone else's plant applies in Form COS-6 and receives Form COS-9 (Rules 23 and 25).
  • The authority is required to decide on an application within forty-five days, and the site may be inspected after grant (Rule 23).
  • A licence stays valid “in perpetuity” as long as the retention fee is paid every five years (Rule 30).
  • The licence holder must test each batch of raw materials and each batch of finished product, and keep the records (Rule 26).

In practice, most first-time brands do not hold any licence of their own. The product is made under the manufacturer's COS-8 licence and the brand appears on the pack as the marketer. Some brands later take a loan licence for more control. We have written a separate, detailed guide on that route: cosmetic loan licence for brand owners.

What your label will show when the product is made in Haryana

Founders are sometimes surprised that the factory's address appears on their pack. It is a legal requirement, not a choice. Rule 34 of the Cosmetics Rules asks for the name of the manufacturer and the “complete address of the premises of the manufacturer where the cosmetic has been manufactured”, along with the manufacturing licence number, the batch number and the use-before or expiry date. Ingredients must be listed in descending order under the word “INGREDIENTS”, with small packs exempted. Separately, the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 govern declarations such as net quantity and retail price on pre-packed goods.

So a serum made for a Gurugram brand at a plant in Ambala will typically read “Marketed by” with the brand's details and “Manufactured by” with the plant's address and licence number. Agree the exact wording early, because label text drives the carton artwork, and artwork is often what holds a launch back. Ask your manufacturer to review the full label against both sets of rules before you approve printing.

Plant visit checklist: what to see on the day

A visit is your chance to judge the operation behind the samples. Ask for a walk-through in the order a product actually moves, from raw material to finished carton, and use the table below as a script. You do not need to be a chemist; you are looking for order, records and straight answers.

The international guideline most plants work towards for good manufacturing practice is ISO 22716, which covers premises, equipment, documentation and complaint handling. You can ask a manufacturer how its own systems map to that guideline. Bring these to the meeting

  1. Two or three benchmark products you admire, opened, so the team can feel what you mean.
  2. Your target selling price and the pack size, since cost and format shape the formula.
  3. A short list of claims you hope to make, so the team can tell you which need test support.
  4. Any packaging you have already sourced, to check it on the filling line.

Planning a launch with a third party cosmetic manufacturer in Haryana

A realistic plan keeps the visits for the moments that need them and runs everything else remotely. A sensible sequence for a three-to-five product range looks like this:

  1. Write the brief. Say whether you want a private label base or a custom formula, then list actives, texture, fragrance direction, claims, target cost, pack and benchmark. A good brief cuts sample rounds more than anything else.
  2. Review first samples by courier. Use them for a week on skin or hair before you comment.
  3. Visit for fine-tuning. Bring your notes and adjust textures at the bench with the formulator.
  4. Lock packaging while stability runs. Tubes, pumps and printed cartons often have the longest lead times, so order them in parallel, not after.
  5. Approve the first batch. Check it against your retained sample, ideally on site.
  6. Plan freight and stock. Decide where stock lands: your warehouse, a fulfilment partner or marketplace warehouses.

Logistics from Ambala to the rest of India

Ambala sits on the main road network linking Delhi, Chandigarh and Punjab, which makes road freight the natural choice for brands across the north. For the rest of the country, consignments move by surface transport or courier like any other factory dispatch. Three questions to settle before your first order ships:

  • Who books freight? Some brands use their own transporter or fulfilment partner; others ask the plant to arrange it. Put the choice and the cost in writing.
  • What documents travel with the goods? Invoice, e-way bill where applicable, and a certificate of analysis for the batch are the usual minimum. Ask for the certificate as a standard, not a favour.
  • How is the stock packed? Shipper cartons, layers per carton and labelling should match what your warehouse or marketplace expects, or you will repack at your cost.

Common mistakes when choosing a nearby plant

  • Choosing on distance alone. A short drive is a bonus, not a substitute for formulation depth, testing and documentation.
  • Skipping the licence check. Ask for the licence copy and confirm your product category is covered before samples begin.
  • Visiting too early or too late. The most useful visit is after the first samples, when there is something concrete to adjust.
  • Leaving packaging to the end. Late packaging is the most common reason a finished formula sits waiting.
  • No written agreement. Formula ownership, pricing, quality responsibilities and freight terms belong on paper before the first batch.

Frequently asked questions

Who issues cosmetic manufacturing licences in Haryana?

Under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, the state drugs controller acts as the State Licensing Authority for cosmetic manufacture. For a plant in Haryana, the licence (Form COS-8) is issued and inspected by the state drug control authority, not by CDSCO in New Delhi.

Do I need my own licence to sell products made by a third party manufacturer?

Usually not for a first launch. The product can be made under the manufacturer's own licence, with your brand shown as the marketer on the label. A loan licence in Form COS-9 is an option if you want your own licence number later.

Can I visit a cosmetic factory before placing an order?

A serious manufacturer should welcome it. Ask to see the raw material store, QC lab, mixing, filling and documents. At Ecosmetix, visits to our Ambala plant can be booked through the contact page or WhatsApp.

How long does it take to get samples from a manufacturer in Haryana?

It depends on the brief and the plant. At Ecosmetix, lab samples are typically ready within 14 days of a clear brief. Stability checks, packaging and production take longer and depend on the product.

Can a Haryana manufacturer supply brands outside north India?

Yes. Location matters most for visits; finished goods move by road freight or courier to any state. Ecosmetix dispatches to brands across India.

Whose address goes on the label when a third party makes my product?

The Cosmetics Rules require the manufacturer's name, the complete address of the premises where the product was made and its manufacturing licence number. Your brand's details usually appear alongside as the marketer.

What should I bring to a first meeting with a cosmetic manufacturer?

Benchmark products you like, your target selling price, preferred pack size, a short list of claims and any packaging you have already sourced. These let the team propose a realistic formula and quote quickly.
