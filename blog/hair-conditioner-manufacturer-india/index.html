Hair Conditioner Manufacturer in India: Formats, Ingredients, Process and What to Ask

24 November 2026 · By the Ecosmetix R&D team

A conditioner is usually the second product a hair care brand adds, and often the one that decides whether customers come back for the shampoo. If you are looking for a hair conditioner manufacturer in India, you are really choosing a partner for a tricky emulsion: it has to detangle wet hair in seconds, rinse clean in hard water, sit well beside your shampoo and still look the same after a hot season in a courier van. This guide is written for founders who want to brief a manufacturer properly and judge the samples that come back.

Why brands add a conditioner, and what a hair conditioner manufacturer does

Shampoo cleans with anionic surfactants, which leave the hair fibre carrying a net negative charge. That charge is behind much of the static, tangling and frizz people notice after washing. A peer-reviewed overview of hair cosmetics in the International Journal of Trichology describes the core action as "neutralizing the electrical negative charge of the hair fiber", with a lubricating effect on the cuticle on top (Gavazzoni Dias, 2015).

For a brand, this makes the conditioner a natural partner product: it lifts basket value, gives the shampoo a better trial experience and opens the door to masks and leave-ins later. A capable partner handles five jobs:

  • Formula work: picking the conditioning agents, fatty phase, actives, fragrance and preservative for your hair-type and price target.
  • Matching: making sure the conditioner pairs with your shampoo in fragrance, colour and claim language.
  • Testing: stability at raised and low temperatures, pack compatibility, pH and viscosity tracking, and microbial checks.
  • Licensed production: manufacturing under a valid licence with batch records, in-process checks and release testing.
  • Filling and dispatch: tubes, bottles or jars filled, coded, cartoned and sent to your warehouse or marketplace hub.

Conditioner formats you can launch

The format decides the base, the pack and the price band. The Trichology overview describes cream rinses as products that "basically contain cationic surfactants, long-chain fatty alcohol or other lipid components", while deep conditioners carry "more concentrated ingredients or just more viscosity". Leave-on products are a separate family designed to stay on the hair. The diagram and table below show how the common formats compare.

What goes inside a conditioner

A conditioner looks simple on the label, yet each building block does a separate job. Knowing them lets you read a formula proposal and ask sharper questions.

Building blockExamples on an INCI listRole in the product
Cationic conditioning agentsBehentrimonium chloride, cetrimonium chloride, behentrimonium methosulfateCarry the positive charge that sticks to hair and reduces static and tangles
Fatty alcoholsCetyl, stearyl or cetearyl alcoholBuild the creamy body and the gel structure; add softness
Cationic polymersPolyquaternium-10, guar hydroxypropyltrimonium chlorideFilm on the hair surface for smoother wet combing
SiliconesDimethicone, amodimethiconeSlip, shine and dry-combing ease; amino types favour damaged areas
Oils and buttersCoconut, argan, olive oil; shea butterSoftness and a richer feel; useful in dry-hair and mask positions
ActivesPanthenol, hydrolysed proteins, keratin, bond-repair ingredientsCarry the product's story and specific claims
Supporting systemPreservatives, pH adjusters, chelating agents, fragranceKeep the product safe, stable and pleasant for its shelf life

The cationic agent and the fatty alcohol together form a structured network in water. The quality of that network is what you feel as "slip" in the shower, and it is also where weak formulas fail: a poorly built base can thin out, separate or turn grainy as it ages. The review points out that dimethicone is "the most widely used silicone in hair care industry", and also that ordinary dimethicone attaches better to healthy hair than to damaged tips, which is why repair formulas often use amino-functional silicones or polymers. A 2023 review in Polymers surveys newer bio-based conditioning agents.

Silicone-free, quat-free and "natural" positions

Many Indian founders want silicone-free conditioners, and some want to avoid quaternium compounds altogether. Both are achievable, but each comes with trade-offs you should hear about before sampling. Without silicones, dry-combing ease and shine have to come from oils, esters and polymers, and the formula needs careful balancing so it does not feel heavy. Without quats, detangling must rely on other conditioning chemistry, and slip on very damaged hair may be lower. Ask for a standard and a free-from sample side by side and judge them on your own hair.

Oil choice also matters for claims. The Trichology review notes that coconut oil can penetrate the hair fibre while mineral oil does not, which is one reason coconut-based conditioners and masks are popular in India.

How a hair conditioner manufacturer takes a project from brief to shelf

The diagram below shows the usual order of work. The timing varies with how many sample rounds you need and how quickly packaging arrives, so treat it as a sequence, not a promise.

  1. Brief. Hair type and concern, the shampoo it should pair with, claims, free-from list, fragrance direction, pack, target retail price and a benchmark product. Our guide to writing a product brief shows what to include.
  2. Lab samples. We typically deliver the first samples within 14 days. Ask for two or three variants that differ in one thing each, such as weight or fragrance level.
  3. Home trial. Use the conditioner after your own shampoo for at least a week. Judge wet slip, rinse time, softness once dry, and whether the hair looks flat by day two.
  4. Stability and pack checks. The final sample is stored at different temperatures in the real pack, with viscosity, pH, colour, odour and appearance tracked.
  5. Pack and label. Artwork is checked against the labelling rules, and the fill line and closure are confirmed.
  6. Production. Batch mixing, in-process checks, filling, coding and release testing.
  7. Dispatch. Cartons go to your warehouse, 3PL or marketplace hub anywhere in India.

Licences and labelling for a conditioner brand in India

A hair conditioner is a cosmetic in India, so it is made under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, which the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) publishes along with its cosmetics guidance. Manufacturing has to happen at premises licensed by the State Licensing Authority. As a brand owner you can sell products made under your manufacturer's licence, or take a loan licence to manufacture at their plant in your own name; our post on the cosmetic loan licence explains the difference in detail.

The label must meet both the Cosmetics Rules and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011. In practice your artwork should carry the product name, the manufacturer's name and address with licence number, net quantity, batch number, manufacturing and expiry or use-before details, maximum retail price, consumer care contact, directions for use, any warnings, and the ingredient list where the rules require it. Finalise artwork only after the formula is frozen.

Claims deserve extra care. Words like "repairs", "strengthens" or "reduces hair fall" need support, and a cosmetic must never claim to treat or cure a medical condition. Keep anti-hair-fall language for the scalp products in your range, and describe what a conditioner does to the hair surface.

Costs, order sizes and timelines

We do not publish a universal price list, because conditioner cost depends on choices you control. The main drivers are:

  • Formula load: a mask with more conditioning agent, butters and actives costs more per kg than a light daily rinse-off.
  • Actives and claims: bond-repair ingredients, proteins and premium oils raise cost, as do any claim tests you commission.
  • Pack: a printed laminate tube, a jar with an inner liner or a trigger spray each change unit cost and minimum purchase quantities.
  • Fragrance: a custom fragrance matched to your shampoo costs more than a library fragrance.
  • Batch size: per-unit cost falls as the batch grows, because set-up, testing and line changeover are spread across more units.

Minimum order quantities are usually set by the packaging supplier and the smallest batch a mixing vessel can run well, rather than by the formula itself. Our guide to cosmetic manufacturing MOQ explains how to plan a first order. Scale is rarely the bottleneck at our Ambala plant: we can mix up to 16,000 kg and fill up to 60,000 tubes per day, which leaves room to grow from a modest first run to repeat orders with the same partner.

Questions to ask any hair conditioner manufacturer

Good partners answer these with specifics.

  • Which conditioning system do you propose for my hair type, and why that one?
  • Can you sample a standard version and a silicone-free or quat-free version together?
  • How will the conditioner be matched to my shampoo in fragrance, colour and pH?
  • What stability programme do you run, at which temperatures, and in which pack?
  • How do you control viscosity from batch to batch, and what range is accepted at release?
  • Which preservative system is used, and has the formula passed a challenge test?
  • Can you share the full INCI list early so we can review it with our label?
  • Which tubes, bottles and jars can your lines fill, and who supplies the packs?
  • Do you hold a valid cosmetic manufacturing licence for this category, and can we see it?

Frequently asked questions

What does a hair conditioner manufacturer need from me to start?

A short brief: the hair type and concern, the shampoo it should pair with, the claims you want, any free-from requirements, fragrance direction, pack type and size, target retail price and one benchmark product. With these, a manufacturer can propose formula routes and send lab samples.

Can I launch a private label hair conditioner without creating my own formula?

Yes. Private label means you choose a proven base from the manufacturer's library and adjust details such as fragrance, actives and pack. It is usually the fastest route to market. At Ecosmetix you can choose from 100+ graded formulas and fine-tune them.

What is the difference between a conditioner and a hair mask?

Both use similar building blocks, but a mask usually carries a richer conditioning load and a thicker texture and is used once or twice a week, often left on for a few minutes. A daily conditioner is lighter so it does not weigh hair down with regular use.

Is a silicone-free conditioner as good as a regular one?

It can perform very well, but it behaves differently. Without silicones, slip and shine come from oils, esters and polymers, and dry-combing ease may be lower on very damaged hair. Ask for a side-by-side sample of both versions and judge them on real hair.

Do I need a separate licence to sell a hair conditioner in India?

A conditioner is a cosmetic, so it must be made at premises licensed under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020. Brand owners usually sell products made under the manufacturer's licence or take a loan licence at the manufacturer's plant. The label must also meet Legal Metrology rules.

Can the conditioner be matched to my existing shampoo?

Yes. A good manufacturer matches fragrance, colour and claim language and checks that the two products work well together in a home trial. We usually sample conditioners alongside the shampoo they will be sold with.

How long does it take to develop a hair conditioner?

At Ecosmetix, first lab samples typically arrive within 14 days of a clear brief. The full launch time then depends on how many sample rounds you need, the stability programme, packaging lead times and artwork approval.

What decides the cost of a third-party hair conditioner?

The formula load (a mask costs more than a light rinse-off), the actives and any claim tests, the pack, the fragrance and the batch size. Per-unit cost usually drops as batch size grows because set-up and testing are spread over more units.
