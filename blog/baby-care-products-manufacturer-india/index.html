Baby Care Products Manufacturer in India: Kids Care Formulas, Safety Rules and Launch Guide

20 November 2026 · By the Ecosmetix R&D team

Parents read a baby product label more carefully than almost any other label in the house, and they come back to a brand they trust for years. That makes baby and kids care one of the most loyal categories in personal care, and it is also why the choice of baby care products manufacturer matters more here than anywhere else. A baby wash or kids shampoo has to be mild, simply built, well preserved and backed by paperwork that a worried parent, a pharmacy buyer or a marketplace can check.

This guide is for founders and brand teams who want to launch baby care or kids care products in India without building their own plant. It explains how products change from newborn to pre-teen, what a careful formula looks like, the Indian rules and standards that apply, the stages of a project, and the questions to ask before you sign. We make a baby care range at our plant in Ambala, Haryana, so we also explain how we approach these projects. Where a point rests on published science or a standard, we link the source.

What a baby care products manufacturer does for your brand

In third party baby care manufacturing, your brand owns the name, the story and the selling. The manufacturer holds the cosmetic manufacturing licence and carries out the technical work. For baby and kids care, that work normally covers:

  • Formula selection or design: a ready, proven base from the manufacturer's library or a custom formula built around your brief.
  • Ingredient screening: checking every raw material, including the fragrance and preservative, against the rules and against your own "free-from" promises.
  • Lab samples for you, your team and a small group of parents to try, side by side with any benchmark you like.
  • Stability, safety and microbiology checks in the actual bottle, tube or jar you plan to sell.
  • Label and claim review, which is stricter for children's products because every word is read as a safety promise.
  • Production: batch mixing, in-process checks, filling, batch coding, cartoning and dispatch.

If you have not yet decided between a ready formula and a new one, our comparison of private label, contract manufacturing and custom formulation will help. For a first baby range, most brands start from proven private label baby care bases and then personalise the fragrance level, the hero botanical and the pack.

Baby, toddler and kids care: products change with age

"Kids care" is not one audience. A newborn, a three-year-old and a ten-year-old have different skin, different routines and different buyers, even though the parent pays in every case. Published research describes infant skin as having a thinner outer layer and higher water loss than adult skin, with the barrier continuing to mature through the first years of life and closely resembling adult skin by around the age of two (Rahma and Lane, 2022). That is the main reason the youngest age band needs the most cautious formulas.

Age bandTypical productsFormulation priorities
Newborn to 12 monthsBaby wash, baby shampoo, massage oil, baby lotion, nappy-area cream, wipes, baby powderFewest possible ingredients, mild cleansing, fragrance-free or very low fragrance, gentle preservation, no harsh surfactants

4 to 8 yearsKids shampoo and conditioner, detangling spray, body wash, kids face wash, mineral sunscreenPlayful sensory cues (colour, mild scent, foam) within safe limits, no adult actives

Choosing the age band first keeps your range honest. A "0+" label promises much more caution than a "3+" or "kids 5 to 12" label, and the formula, testing plan and claims must match the youngest age you print on the pack. How a careful baby care formula is built

The best baby products are not the ones with the longest ingredient list. A European expert roundtable on healthy infant skin care (Blume-Peytavi and colleagues, 2016) set out what parents and industry should aim for. These are the points our formulators treat as the minimum standard for any baby care brief:

1. Mild cleansing at a skin-friendly pH

The roundtable advises against soap-based cleansers with a pH around 10 and prefers mild synthetic detergent (syndet) liquid cleansers that are neutral to mildly acidic, roughly pH 5.5 to 7.0. Harsh surfactants such as sodium lauryl sulphate are best avoided. In practice that means blends of gentle amphoteric and non-ionic surfactants, often with a polymer that reduces irritation. Our baby care range is built on gentle surfactants for exactly this reason.

2. Leave-on products that respect the skin's surface

Products that stay on the skin, such as lotions, creams and oils, should not disturb the skin surface. The same paper recommends that leave-on baby products help keep the skin surface at about pH 5.5, and a review on the infant skin barrier makes the same point about not interfering with skin pH (Telofski and colleagues, 2012). Natural butters, plant oils chosen for oxidation stability, glycerin and simple emollients are the usual building blocks.

3. Fragrance and colour: less is more

Fragrance is one of the most common reasons for skin reactions from cosmetics. For newborn products we recommend fragrance-free versions, or a very low level of a fragrance chosen for low allergy risk, and no added colour. For older kids a mild, pleasant scent helps bath-time compliance, but it still needs to be used at a low level and screened for listed allergens.

4. Preservation that is effective and well tolerated

Water-based baby products must be protected against microbes for the whole shelf life and the whole period after opening. "Preservative-free" is rarely a safe promise for a water-based product. The better route is a preservative system with a long safety record in baby products, at the lowest effective level, confirmed by a preservative efficacy (challenge) test. Methylisothiazolinone, for example, is linked to contact allergy in children and is a poor choice for this category.

5. Packaging that protects the child and the product

Pumps and flip caps reduce contamination compared with open jars, and tubes protect creams from air. For kids, think about caps a toddler cannot easily chew off, and about bottles that are easy for one hand to use at bath time.

Rules and standards for baby care products in India

Baby and kids products sold as cosmetics come under the Drugs and Cosmetics Act, 1940 and the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, regulated by the Central Drugs Standard Control Organisation (CDSCO) and the State Licensing Authorities. The product must be made under a valid cosmetic manufacturing licence. A brand can sell under its manufacturer's licence or take a loan licence on the manufacturer's premises.

The Bureau of Indian Standards (BIS) publishes product standards that Indian manufacturers use as a quality reference. Two are useful for this category:

  • IS 5339:2004, Skin powder for infants: sets a pH of 5.5 to 8.0 for the powder's aqueous suspension, requires the powder to be free from boric acid, and sets a tight limit on microbial count with Gram-negative pathogens absent. Its foreword notes that many manufacturers keep the formula as simple as possible.
  • IS 6608:2004, Skin creams: covers moisturising, emollient and general-purpose creams, with requirements such as pH range, microbial limits and dermatological safety testing.

Labels must also meet the Cosmetics Rules labelling requirements and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules for net quantity, MRP, manufacturer details and consumer care information. For children's products, be strict about claims. Words like "safe for newborns", "tear-free", "hypoallergenic" or "dermatologically tested" need test data behind them, and anything that promises to treat a condition, such as eczema or nappy rash as a disease, moves a product towards drug rules. Our guide to choosing a third party manufacturer lists the documents to request.

If you plan to export, note that rules elsewhere can be stricter. In the EU, for example, products meant for children under three need a specific safety assessment, and the EU's scientific committee has reviewed ingredients such as salicylic acid specifically for children's exposure.

The baby care product range most brands launch with

A first range works best when it covers one complete routine rather than many unrelated products. A typical starter range looks like this:

  1. Baby wash or top-to-toe wash: mild syndet base, low or no fragrance, pump bottle.
  2. Baby shampoo: very mild foam, designed to be gentle around the eyes; any "tear-free" claim must be supported by eye-irritation data.
  3. Baby lotion: light emulsion with butters and glycerin, quick to spread, non-greasy for Indian summers.
  4. Baby massage oil: a cosmetic-grade oil blend chosen for stability, a long-standing part of baby care in Indian homes. The roundtable advises appropriately formulated baby oils rather than kitchen oils.
  5. Nappy-area cream: a barrier cream, often with zinc oxide, positioned as protective care rather than a treatment.
  6. Winter or dry-skin cream: a richer cream for north Indian winters.

For kids aged 4 to 12, brands often add a kids shampoo and conditioner, a detangling spray, a fun-scent body wash and a mineral sunscreen. Our sunscreen manufacturing team can advise on mineral options for children; for babies under six months, paediatric advice generally favours shade and clothing over sunscreen.

Questions to ask a baby care products manufacturer

  • Which baby and kids formulas do you already make, and which age band was each designed for?
  • What surfactant system do you use in baby washes, and what is the finished pH?
  • Which preservative system will you use, and will you run a preservative efficacy test in my final pack?
  • Can you supply fragrance-free versions, and allergen information for any fragrance you add?
  • What safety testing supports claims like "dermatologically tested" or "tear-free"?
  • How do you check stability and compatibility with my bottle, pump or tube?
  • Which licence will the product be made under, and will you review my label against the Cosmetics Rules and Legal Metrology rules?
  • What is the minimum batch, and how many filled units does it give in my chosen pack size?

Clear answers to these questions protect your brand more than any marketing claim. Visit our skin care manufacturing page or third party cosmetic manufacturing page to see how we work.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start a baby care brand in India?

Choose your age band and a small routine of three to six products, write a brief, and work with a licensed baby care products manufacturer to sample, test and stabilise the formulas. Get your labels and claims reviewed, then start with a first batch you can sell through.

Do I need a licence to sell baby care products in India?

Baby cosmetics must be made under a cosmetic manufacturing licence under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020. A brand can sell under its manufacturer's licence or take a loan licence on the manufacturer's premises, and labels must meet cosmetic and Legal Metrology rules.

What pH should a baby wash have?

An expert roundtable on infant skin care recommends neutral to mildly acidic liquid cleansers, roughly pH 5.5 to 7.0, and advises avoiding soap-based cleansers with a pH around 10.

Can baby products be preservative-free?

Water-based baby products need protection against microbes, so a preservative-free claim is rarely safe. A well-tolerated preservative at the lowest effective level, confirmed by a challenge test, is the better choice. Anhydrous products such as oils and balms can avoid water-phase preservatives.

What does tear-free mean on a baby shampoo?

It means the shampoo is formulated with very mild surfactants to reduce stinging if it gets into the eyes. The claim should be supported by eye-irritation test data before it is printed.

Can kids use adult skincare products?

Children's skin does not need adult actives such as retinoids or strong exfoliating acids. Kids care products should be simple, mild and designed for the youngest age printed on the pack.

What is the minimum order for private label baby care products?

It depends on the manufacturer, the product and the pack size. Ask for the minimum batch in kilograms and how many filled units it gives in your chosen bottle or tube.
