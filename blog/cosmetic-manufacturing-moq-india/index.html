Cosmetic Manufacturing MOQ in India: What Sets It and How to Plan Your First Order

23 November 2026 · By the Ecosmetix R&D team

Almost every first conversation between a new brand and a factory reaches the same question within ten minutes: what is the minimum we have to order? The cosmetic manufacturing MOQ decides how much cash you lock into stock before a single customer has paid, how many SKUs you can afford at launch and how long that stock must sell before it nears expiry. Yet most founders receive a single number on a quote with no explanation of where it came from, so they cannot tell whether it is fixed or negotiable.

This guide unpacks the minimum order quantity from the factory floor outwards. We explain what actually sets it, how to read a quote so that hidden minimums do not surprise you, how private label and custom formulation change the picture, and a practical plan for a first order that is small enough to be safe and large enough to be produced properly.

What cosmetic manufacturing MOQ actually means

An MOQ is not one figure. When a manufacturer says "minimum order", it may be talking about any of four different things, and two quotes that look alike can mean very different commitments:

  • Bulk quantity: the smallest batch, in kilograms or litres, that the plant will mix for one formula. This is the true production minimum.
  • Filled units: the number of finished tubes, bottles or jars that the bulk turns into. The same bulk gives very different unit counts at 30 ml and at 200 ml.
  • Per SKU: most minimums apply to each formula and pack combination separately. Three face washes in one size are three minimums, not one.
  • Per variant: a change of fragrance, colour or active level usually counts as a new formula, so it carries its own minimum even when the base is shared.

Five things that set a cosmetic manufacturing MOQ

A factory does not choose its minimum at random. Several separate limits sit behind it, and the largest of them becomes the number on your quote. Knowing which one is binding tells you where there is room to move.

1. The mixing vessel

Every mixer has a lowest working volume below which the blades or homogeniser head do not sit properly in the product. Run it too empty and an emulsion may not form a stable droplet size, air gets whipped in and heating becomes uneven. Ask which vessel your product would go into and what its practical lower limit is.

2. Raw material pack sizes

Speciality actives, emulsifiers and fragrances are sold by suppliers in fixed packs. If a rare active is only available in a pack far larger than your batch needs, the leftover either sits in the store until it expires or is charged to you.

3. Packaging components

Tubes, bottles, pumps, caps, labels and cartons each come from a separate supplier with its own minimum. Custom moulds, a special colour or direct printing push those minimums up sharply. In our experience this is the limit that most often decides the real order size for a new brand, which is why it has its own section below.

4. Set-up, cleaning and line change

Before and after each batch the vessel, pipes and filling heads must be cleaned, the filler set to the new pack and the line cleared of the previous product's labels. That effort is roughly the same for a short run as for a long one, so very small batches carry a heavy share of it per unit.

5. Testing and batch release

Each batch needs its own quality checks before release, such as appearance, odour, pH, viscosity and microbial limits where relevant, plus retained samples and a batch record. GMP guidance such as ISO 22716 expects this for every batch, whatever its size.

Reading a quote: quoted MOQ versus practical MOQ

The figure written on a quotation is often lower than what you will actually pay for once packaging and add-ons are counted. Use the questions below when you review any offer for third party cosmetic manufacturing.

Question to askWhy it matters
Is the minimum per formula, per SKU or per total order?A per-SKU minimum multiplies with every size and variant you add.
Is it stated in kilograms of bulk or in filled units?You need both numbers to compare offers and plan cash flow.
Does packaging carry its own minimum, and who buys it?Surplus tubes or printed cartons may become your stock and your cost.
Can one bulk batch be filled into two pack sizes?Splitting a batch lets you launch a travel size without a second mix.
What happens to leftover bulk after filling?Some loss in pipes and the hopper is normal; agree how it is handled.
Are stability checks and the batch certificate included?Testing billed separately changes the real cost of a small first run.

Worked example: from batch size to finished units

Founders often think in units while factories plan in kilograms, so it helps to translate between the two. The table below is plain arithmetic for a single 100 kg batch, before allowing for filling losses. It is an illustration of the conversion, not an Ecosmetix price or minimum.

Fill sizeUnits from a 100 kg batch (before losses)What it suggests
30 g serum or gelAbout 3,333Small packs turn a modest batch into a large unit count
50 g tube2,000A common size for creams and face washes
100 g tube1,000Fewer units, but each carries more bulk
200 g bottle500Body and hair products reach the minimum in fewer units

Liquids filled by volume need one extra step: multiply the fill volume by the product's density to get grams per unit. The lesson is that the same batch size can feel huge for a serum and modest for a shampoo, so plan your launch quantities product by product.

How private label and custom formulas change the minimum

The route you choose to develop a product affects the minimum more than most founders expect.

  • Private label (ready formulas): the formula already exists, has been made at scale and has a stability history. You choose from a library, adjust fragrance or pack and put your brand on it. Because development risk is low, the conversation about a private label skin care MOQ usually centres on packaging rather than on the bulk.
  • Fully custom formulation: a new product built from your brief. It needs development, sampling rounds, stability studies and sometimes speciality raw materials, so the first commercial batch usually has to justify that investment.

Our private label service draws on a library of 100+ formulas graded from Grade 1 to Grade 4 by how advanced the actives and delivery are, so founders can start with something proven and move to custom work once a product finds its buyers. For a broader comparison, read private label vs contract manufacturing vs custom formulation.

Packaging MOQ: often the real bottleneck

For a first order, the packaging MOQ very often outweighs the bulk minimum. A factory can mix a short batch, but a tube maker printing your artwork in six colours will only set up the press for a large run. Some practical ways around this:

  • Start with stock components. Standard tubes, bottles and pumps in common colours are held in inventory by suppliers and can be bought in smaller lots.
  • Label rather than print. A good pressure-sensitive label on a stock tube or bottle looks premium and lets you change copy between batches without scrapping printed stock.
  • Delay custom moulds. Bespoke shapes make sense once a product has proved itself and the volume justifies the tooling.

Remember that whoever buys the packaging owns any surplus. Agree in writing whether leftover printed stock is held for your next batch, and for how long.

Five ways to lower your first order safely

A low MOQ is only useful if the product is still made properly. These steps reduce your commitment without cutting corners on quality:

  1. Launch with three to five hero SKUs instead of a full range, and add variants once sales data shows what moves.
  2. Choose sizes that use the batch efficiently, and consider filling one batch into a full size and a trial size.
  3. Prefer ready or lightly customised formulas for the first launch and keep fully custom work for the second wave.
  4. Use stock packaging with labels for the first run and move to printed or custom packs on reorder.
  5. Share a realistic twelve-month forecast; a manufacturer can plan raw materials better when it can see your second and third batch.

If you are searching for a low MOQ cosmetic manufacturer, judge any offer by what stays in place at the smaller size: batch records, release testing, stability support and the same quality checks as a large run. A small batch made without those is not a bargain.

Rules that apply to every batch, however small

A smaller order does not mean lighter compliance. Whatever the quantity, plan for the following:

  • Manufacturing licence: under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, cosmetics must be made in premises licensed by the State Licensing Authority. When a brand uses a third-party plant, a loan licence or a written arrangement with the licensed manufacturer is needed; our guide to the cosmetic loan licence explains the options.
  • Batch identity on the label: the labelling provisions of the Cosmetics Rules expect each pack to carry details such as the batch number, manufacturing licence number and dates, so every batch must be traceable.
  • Legal Metrology declarations: the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 set out the declarations a retail pack must show, including net quantity, the manufacturer or packer's name and address, the month and year of manufacture, the retail sale price and consumer care details.
  • Plastic packaging EPR: brand owners placing plastic packaging on the market generally need to register under the Extended Producer Responsibility framework, which applies from the first batch.
  • Batch records: good manufacturing practice expects a full record of what went into each batch and how it was checked before release.

Please confirm the current requirements for your product with the regulator or a qualified consultant, since rules are amended from time to time.

Frequently asked questions

What is MOQ in cosmetic manufacturing?

It is the smallest quantity a manufacturer will produce for one formula in one run. It can be stated as kilograms of bulk or as finished units, and it usually applies to each SKU separately, so always ask which of these the number on your quote refers to.

Is the minimum order quantity per product or per order?

In most cases it applies per formula and pack combination. Two sizes of one face wash, or one cream in two fragrances, are normally treated as separate SKUs, each with its own minimum. Some plants allow one bulk batch to be split across pack sizes, which is worth asking about.

Can a new brand start with a low MOQ in India?

Yes, if the plan is built for it. Ready or lightly customised formulas, stock packaging with labels and a focused range of a few SKUs all help keep the first order small. The trade-off is usually a higher cost per unit than a large run.

Why is the MOQ higher for a custom formula?

A custom formula needs development, sampling rounds, stability checks and sometimes speciality raw materials that are sold only in large packs. The first commercial batch has to carry that investment, so minimums for new formulas are often higher than for proven ready formulas.

Does the MOQ include packaging?

Not always. Tubes, bottles, pumps, labels and cartons have their own supplier minimums, which can be larger than the bulk minimum, especially for printed or custom items. Check who buys the packaging and who owns any surplus.

Can one batch be filled into two pack sizes?

Often it can, as long as both packs are compatible with the formula and the filling line. This lets a brand launch a full size and a travel or trial size from one batch. Confirm it with the manufacturer before the batch is scheduled.

Does a small batch need the same testing and licences?

Yes. Licensing, labelling, Legal Metrology declarations, batch records and release testing apply to every batch regardless of its size. A smaller order changes the quantity, not the compliance work.
