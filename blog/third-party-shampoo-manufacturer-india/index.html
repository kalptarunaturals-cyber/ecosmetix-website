Third Party Shampoo Manufacturer in India: Formulas, Process, Rules and Questions to Ask

19 November 2026 · By the Ecosmetix R&D team

Most Indian hair care brands do not own a factory. They work with a third party shampoo manufacturer that develops the formula, runs stability checks, fills the bottles and dispatches finished stock, while the brand concentrates on positioning, content and sales. Choosing that partner well is the single biggest decision in a shampoo launch, because the surfactant system, the pH and the preservative choice decide how the product feels in the shower and whether it survives a summer warehouse in Nagpur.

This guide walks through what a founder needs to know before signing with a manufacturer: shampoo types and what each needs, how the formula is built, the development and production timeline, the Indian rules that apply, the questions worth asking, and how we handle shampoo projects at our plant in Ambala, Haryana.

What a third party shampoo manufacturer actually does

A good partner does far more than mix and fill. In a typical project the manufacturer takes responsibility for:

  • Formulation: choosing the surfactant blend, conditioning polymers, actives, fragrance and preservative to match your brief and target cost.
  • Sampling: lab batches you can use at home, share with a test panel and compare against your benchmark.
  • Stability and compatibility: checking that viscosity, colour, odour and pH hold up over time and in the chosen bottle.
  • Licensing and compliance support: producing under a valid state manufacturing licence and helping you check label declarations.
  • Production and quality control: batch manufacture, in-process checks, filling, coding and release testing.
  • Dispatch: packed cartons sent to your warehouse, fulfilment partner or marketplace hub.

Some brands come with a fixed formula and only need filling. Most first-time founders, though, want a partner that can either offer a proven base (private label) or build something new (custom formulation). If you are still deciding between those routes, our pillar page on hair care manufacturing and the broader third-party manufacturing page explain how each one works.

A note on medicated products: a shampoo that claims to treat a disease of the scalp may fall outside cosmetic rules altogether. Keep claims cosmetic (cleansing, reducing visible flakes, softness, shine) unless you are deliberately building a drug product with the right licence.

How a shampoo formula is built

A peer-reviewed overview in the Indian Journal of Dermatology notes that modern shampoos usually combine two to four surfactants, with the first one listed acting as the main cleanser (D’Souza and Rathi, 2015). Around that core sit the other building blocks:

  • Primary surfactant: usually anionic. Strong at removing sebum, but harsh on its own.
  • Secondary surfactants: amphoteric types such as cocamidopropyl betaine soften the blend and improve lather; non-ionic types add mildness.
  • Conditioning agents: cationic polymers and, where wanted, silicones that deposit on the hair and cut friction.
  • Thickeners and pearlisers: they set the viscosity and appearance customers judge in the first second.
  • Chelating agent: binds calcium and magnesium so hard water does not leave dull residue. This matters a lot in many Indian cities.
  • pH adjuster, preservative and fragrance: the quiet ingredients that decide comfort, shelf life and repeat purchase.
  • Hero actives: botanical extracts, panthenol, peptides, anti-dandruff agents. The same review cautions that many additives get only brief scalp contact in a rinse-off product, so set honest expectations.

Why pH deserves a line in your brief

A 2014 study in the International Journal of Trichology measured 123 shampoos and found pH values from 3.5 to 9.0, with about 62% above 5.5. The authors linked alkaline products to more negative static charge on the fibre, more friction and more frizz (Gavazzoni Dias et al., 2014). That is a useful reason to state a target pH range in your brief rather than leaving it to chance.

Shampoo manufacturing process and timeline

Once the brief is clear, a shampoo project moves through six stages. The slowest part is usually not the lab; it is packaging lead time and the brand’s own approval rounds.

A few practical points on each stage:

  1. Brief: name the shampoo type, hair concern, must-have and must-avoid ingredients, target cost per unit, pack size and one benchmark product you like. Our guide on writing a cosmetic product brief has a template.
  2. Concept: the chemist proposes one or two surfactant routes. Ask why each was chosen, not just what it costs.
  3. Sample: test with real users in your target city. Hard water in one region and soft water in another can change lather noticeably.
  4. Approve: lock the formula only after stability observations in the actual bottle. A clear shampoo can turn hazy, and a viscous one can thin out, once it sits in contact with the plastic.
  5. Pack: flip-top caps suit thicker bases, pumps suit lower viscosity. Confirm the closure does not leak in transit.
  6. Produce: agree release specifications (appearance, odour, pH range, viscosity range, microbial limits) in writing before the first batch.

Rules that apply to shampoo made in India

Shampoo is a cosmetic under Indian law, so three sets of requirements shape every pack.

Licence. According to CDSCO, cosmetic manufacture is regulated through “inspection and licensing by the State Licensing Authorities”. Your manufacturer must hold a valid state manufacturing licence covering shampoo. If you want the licence in your own name while using their plant, read our explainer on the cosmetic loan licence.

Product standard. Rule 39 of the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, as quoted on the CDSCO cosmetics page, requires every cosmetic made or imported here to meet the specifications “prescribed under the Ninth Schedule”. For shampoo, the Bureau of Indian Standards publishes IS 7884, currently the fourth revision of 2023. Ask your manufacturer which edition they test against and request the batch test report.

Claims and labels. CDSCO also notes that Rule 36 bars claims that are false or misleading. Phrases such as “stops hair fall permanently” or “cures dandruff” invite trouble. On the pack, declarations such as net quantity, the maker’s name and address, and consumer care details are governed by the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011, alongside the cosmetic labelling rules.

We are a manufacturer, not a law firm. Use this as a map and confirm the final label with a regulatory consultant.

Questions to ask a third party shampoo manufacturer

Use this checklist on your first call. A capable partner answers most of it without hesitation.

  • Which state manufacturing licence do you hold, and does it list shampoo?
  • Can I see a recent batch test report against IS 7884?
  • Do you have ready shampoo bases, or will this be a new formula? Who owns the formula afterwards?
  • What is the minimum order quantity for my pack size, and does it change for a custom formula versus a ready base?
  • How many sample rounds are included, and what is the typical sampling time?
  • How do you check stability, and in which conditions?
  • Which preservative system do you propose, and is it suitable for the claims on my label (for example, “paraben-free”)?
  • Can the same base give me a matching conditioner or mask so my range is consistent?
  • What filling formats do you run: bottles, tubes, sachets, pumps?
  • What happens if a batch fails release testing?
  • Can I visit the plant and attend the first production batch?

For a wider version of this checklist covering any category, see how to choose a third-party cosmetic manufacturer.

Private label shampoo vs custom formula: which suits a first launch?

A private label shampoo starts from a base the manufacturer has already developed and tested. You choose the variant, fragrance and pack, and the timeline is shorter. A custom formula is built around your brief and positioning, which takes longer but gives you something competitors cannot simply order.

Many brands combine the two: they launch with one or two proven bases to start selling, then commission a custom hero product once they have customer feedback. For a range plan, think in systems rather than single bottles. A shampoo, conditioner and leave-in serum built on compatible chemistry feel deliberate, and they raise basket size.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start my own shampoo brand in India?

Decide your shampoo type and customer first, then write a brief with claims, target cost, pack size and a benchmark. Shortlist licensed manufacturers, compare samples on real users, lock the formula after stability checks, finalise compliant labels and plan a small first batch. Most founders work with a third party shampoo manufacturer rather than building a plant.

What is the minimum order quantity for third party shampoo manufacturing?

There is no single industry figure. MOQ depends on whether you use a ready base or a custom formula, the batch size of the mixing vessel, the pack size and the bottle supplier's own minimums. Ask each manufacturer for MOQ per SKU and per pack size in writing.

Can a manufacturer make a sulphate-free shampoo that still foams well?

Yes. Sulphate-free shampoos replace the usual anionic sulphate with milder surfactant combinations, often supported by amphoteric surfactants such as betaines. Foam can be close to a conventional shampoo, but feel and lather in hard water should be checked on samples before you approve.

What pH should a shampoo have?

There is no single legal target in the brief, but a 2014 study in the International Journal of Trichology linked lower-pH shampoos with less static and frizz, and a dermatology review recommends not exceeding about pH 5.5. Agree a pH range with your manufacturer and include it in the release specification.

Which BIS standard applies to shampoo?

The Bureau of Indian Standards publishes IS 7884 for shampoo; the current edition is the fourth revision of 2023. Ask your manufacturer which edition their test reports refer to.

Do I need my own licence to sell shampoo made by a third party manufacturer?

Not always. Products can be made under the manufacturer's state licence with your brand shown as the marketer, or you can hold a loan licence in your own name for their site. Each route changes what goes on the label, so confirm with your State Licensing Authority.

How long does it take to develop a shampoo?

At Ecosmetix, lab samples usually arrive within 14 days of a clear brief. The full launch timeline also depends on revision rounds, stability observation, packaging lead times and artwork approval.

Can I claim that my shampoo stops hair fall or cures dandruff?

Avoid it. Rule 36 of the Cosmetics Rules, 2020 bars false or misleading claims, and cure-type wording moves a product towards drug territory. Use cosmetic claims such as reducing visible flakes or breakage from combing, and keep evidence on file.
