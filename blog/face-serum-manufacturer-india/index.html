Face Serum Manufacturer in India: Formats, Actives, Process and Questions to Ask

20 November 2026 · By the Ecosmetix R&D team

A good-looking dropper bottle is easy to buy. A serum that stays stable, feels right on humid Indian skin and actually delivers its active is much harder, and that is why the choice of face serum manufacturer decides most of a launch. This guide is written for founders who want to put a vitamin C, niacinamide, peptide or acne serum on the market without owning a plant. It covers serum formats, actives and the trade-offs behind them, the stages of a project, the paperwork, and the questions to put to any manufacturer before you sign.

We make serums for brands at our plant in Ambala, Haryana, so we have also explained how we run these projects. Where a point depends on law or published science, we have linked the source.

What a face serum manufacturer does for your brand

In third party serum manufacturing, your brand owns the name, the positioning and the selling, while the manufacturer holds the licence to make the product and carries out the technical work. For serums that work usually includes:

  • Formula design: choosing the base, the active level, the pH window, the preservative system and, where useful, a delivery system.
  • Lab samples you can test on yourself, your team and a small panel, side by side with any benchmark you admire.
  • Stability and pack checks: watching colour, smell, viscosity, pH and separation over time, inside the actual bottle and dropper you plan to sell.
  • Regulatory support: making the product under a valid manufacturing licence and reviewing the ingredient list and label text with you.
  • Production: batch mixing, in-process and release checks, filling, batch coding, cartoning and dispatch.

If you are still deciding whether you want a ready formula or a new one, read our comparison of private label, contract manufacturing and custom formulation first. The rest of this article assumes you have picked a direction and want to know what a serum project really involves.

Serum formats: choose the base before the active

Founders usually start with an ingredient ("a 10% niacinamide serum"). Formulators start with the base, because the base decides how much of the active can be carried, how it feels, and which bottle will protect it. Our lab works with 15 to 20 texture types, from bi-phasic serums to cooling fluids, and the main serum families look like this:

Serum formatTypical feelSuitsWatch out for
Water-light (aqueous)Thin, quick to absorbNiacinamide, hyaluronic acid, peptidesTackiness at high humectant levels
Gel serumCushiony, slightly thickerOily and acne-prone skin, soothing activesThickener choice can clash with acids or salts
Milky emulsion serumSoft, lotion-likeRetinoids, ceramides, dry skinNeeds a more complex preservative and stability plan
Bi-phasic (shake before use)Oil layer over water layerBlends of oil-soluble and water-soluble activesClear instructions on the pack; layers must re-separate cleanly

Match the format to the person you are selling to. A gel or water-light base usually fits a young, oily-skinned buyer in a hot city; a milky serum fits a mature, dry-skinned buyer who uses it at night.

Actives and the questions behind each one

A serum label invites comparison on two numbers: the active and its percentage. A responsible face serum manufacturer will talk you through what that number does and does not mean.

Vitamin C

Pure L-ascorbic acid is the most researched form but it oxidises readily and turns yellow or brown in the bottle. A 2025 review of topical vitamin C in the Journal of Skin and Stem Cell compared it with derivatives such as magnesium ascorbyl phosphate, sodium ascorbyl phosphate and ascorbyl tetraisopalmitate, and concluded that "no single derivative is universally superior". In practice the choice is a trade between potency, stability, skin feel and cost, and it changes the packaging you need: airless or opaque packs are worth discussing for any vitamin C product.

Niacinamide

Niacinamide is popular because it is water-soluble, fairly stable and suits oily, acne-prone and uneven-toned skin. Higher percentages are not automatically better; some buyers find very high levels uncomfortable. Published dermatology reviews of nicotinamide discuss both its uses and its side effects, and your manufacturer should help you choose a level you can defend on the pack.

Retinoids, peptides and newer actives

Retinol and bakuchiol benefit from slow-release carriers; we use microsponge technology for exactly this purpose. Peptides are usually gentle but need the right pH and preservative pairing. Newer bio-actives such as exosomes and PDRN raise the question of how a claim will be supported, so plan the evidence along with the formula.

Acne and pigmentation actives

Salicylic acid, azelaic acid and tranexamic derivatives each have their own pH and solubility demands. Encapsulated or pod-based delivery (our Azelaic Z-Pods are one example) can lower the sting while keeping the active on the skin longer. Our guide to delivery systems explains how liposomes, microsponges and encapsulation differ.

How a face serum manufacturer runs the project, stage by stage

Most delays in serum launches happen between stages, when a decision is waiting on the brand. Knowing the sequence helps you keep things moving.

  1. Brief. Share the skin concern, target buyer, hero active and level, texture, pack size, retail price and a benchmark. Our product brief guide lists what to include.
  2. Concept. R&D proposes a base, a delivery route and a realistic active level for your cost.
  3. Lab sample. We typically ship first samples within 14 days of an approved brief.
  4. Feedback and lock. Use the sample for at least a week. Give written feedback on feel, smell, spreading, finish and any tingling, and keep track of the revision number you approve.
  5. Stability and compatibility. The locked formula goes into your chosen bottle, dropper or pump and is checked at different temperatures over time.
  6. Artwork and labelling. Ingredient list, net quantity, batch and date details and directions are checked against the rules before printing.
  7. Production. The batch is mixed, tested, filled, coded and packed. Our plant mixes up to 16,000 kg a day and fills up to 60,000 tubes a day, so a reorder does not wait on capacity.
  8. Dispatch to your warehouse or fulfilment partner anywhere in India.

Private label or custom serum: picking the route

The fastest path is a ready base from an existing range. Our private label library holds more than 100 formulas graded from Grade 1 (daily care) to Grade 4 (advanced correction), so a founder can pick a proven serum, adjust fragrance, colour or texture, and move on to packaging. A custom serum takes longer but lets you own a formula built around your actives, levels and claims.

A sensible plan for many first-time brands is to launch one hero serum on a ready base, learn from real buyers, and fund a custom second-generation serum from those sales. The pillar page on skin care manufacturing lists the categories our graded range covers.

Licences, labels and claims for serums in India

Serums are cosmetics in India as long as they are meant to cleanse, beautify or alter appearance, and not to treat disease. Three sets of rules matter most:

  • Manufacturing licence. Under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, manufacture is licensed by the State Licensing Authority; CDSCO's cosmetics page explains the framework. Check that your manufacturer's licence is current and covers the category, or that you hold a loan licence on their premises. Our loan licence guide covers that route.
  • Label rules. The Cosmetics Rules set the cosmetic label requirements, and the Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules govern declarations such as net quantity, maximum retail price and the name and address of the manufacturer and marketer.
  • Claims. Words such as "cures", "heals" or "treats" push a product towards drug territory. Keep claims cosmetic, and back performance wording with tests: user trials or dermatological testing, planned with the manufacturer at the formula stage.

This is a summary for planning, not legal advice; confirm details with your regulatory consultant before artwork is final.

Checklist: questions to ask a face serum manufacturer

  • Which licence will the serum be made under, and can I see a copy?
  • What base and pH range do you propose, and why does it suit my active?
  • How will you protect an unstable active: packaging, derivatives, encapsulation?
  • How many sample revisions are included before I pay for further rounds?
  • Which stability checks will you run in my final pack, and what results will I receive?
  • Who owns a custom formula, and is exclusivity agreed in writing?
  • What is the serum minimum order quantity for my pack size, in kilograms and in filled units, and what is the lead time for a reorder?
  • Which release tests does each batch go through, and do I get a certificate of analysis?
  • Can you support the claims I want to print, and what testing would that need?

Answers to these tell you more than a price list. A partner that answers specifically, in writing, is usually the one that will handle problems well later.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start a face serum brand in India?

Pick one skin concern and one buyer, then write a brief with the hero active, texture, pack size, retail price and a benchmark. Work with a licensed face serum manufacturer to sample, lock and stability-check the formula, get your labels reviewed, and start with a modest first batch you can sell through.

What is the minimum order quantity for a private label serum?

It depends on the manufacturer, the pack size and whether the formula is ready or custom. Ask for the minimum batch in kilograms and the number of filled units it gives in your chosen bottle, since both figures affect your first investment.

How long does it take to make a face serum with a third party manufacturer?

At Ecosmetix, first lab samples are typically ready within 14 days of an approved brief. Feedback rounds, stability checks in the final pack, packaging supply and artwork approval add time, so plan the full launch around those stages as well.

Which serum is best to launch first?

Choose the serum that solves a clear problem for your target buyer and that you can explain simply, such as niacinamide for oily skin or a gentle vitamin C for dullness. A ready, proven base lowers the risk of a first launch.

Do I need a licence to sell a face serum in India?

The product must be made under a cosmetic manufacturing licence issued by the State Licensing Authority under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020. A brand can rely on its manufacturer's licence or take a loan licence on the manufacturer's premises, and the label must meet cosmetic and Legal Metrology rules.

Why does vitamin C serum change colour?

L-ascorbic acid oxidises when exposed to air, light and heat, and the colour shift from pale to yellow or brown is a sign of this. Choice of derivative, pH, antioxidants and airless or opaque packaging all help slow it down.

Can a manufacturer copy a serum I like?

A good manufacturer can match the texture and feel of a benchmark and then differentiate it with your own actives or delivery system. Copying another brand's formula, name or pack design is not something to ask for.
