Under Eye Cream Manufacturer in India: Concerns, Formats, Testing and Claims

25 November 2026 · By the Ecosmetix R&D team

Eye care is one of the few skin care categories where shoppers expect a product of its own rather than a spoonful of their face cream. That makes it a smart second or third launch for a growing brand. If you are looking for an under eye cream manufacturer in India, the real work is not finding someone who can fill a tube. It is finding a team that understands why the skin around the eyes behaves differently, which concern your product is meant to address, and how to keep the formula gentle, stable and honest on the label. This guide walks Indian founders through each of those decisions, step by step.

What an under eye cream manufacturer does for your brand

A capable partner covers far more than mixing and filling. For a typical eye care launch you should expect help with six stages:

  • Concern mapping: agreeing whether the hero benefit is dark circles, puffiness, fine lines, dryness or a mix, because each one points to a different set of actives and textures.
  • Formulation: choosing actives, their levels, the base (cream, gel or serum), the preservative system and whether fragrance is left out.
  • Sampling and revisions: lab samples you can try and share with a small panel, then rounds of feedback.
  • Testing: stability, pack compatibility, microbiology and eye-area safety work before anything is printed.
  • Licensed production: manufacturing under a valid cosmetic manufacturing licence, with batch records and release checks.
  • Filling, coding and dispatch: tubes, airless pumps or roll-ons filled, batch coded, cartoned and sent to your warehouse.

If a supplier only talks about price per piece and never asks what the product should do, treat that as a warning sign. Eye products leave little room for a generic base with a new name on it.

Start with the concern, not the ingredient list

Many first briefs begin with a list of fashionable actives. A better starting point is the problem your customer sees in the mirror. Dark circles in particular are not one condition. A review in the Journal of Drugs in Dermatology describes periorbital hyperpigmentation as a "final common pathway" of several underlying factors, including dermatitis, allergy, sleep disturbance and nutritional deficiency, and notes that sun protection is central to managing it (Roberts, 2014). For a product team, the practical lesson is simple: one cream will not solve every type of dark circle, so pick a lane and write claims that fit it.

Pick one row as your hero and at most one supporting benefit. A product that claims to fix all five usually ends up doing none of them well, and it becomes very hard to support the claims later.

Formats you can launch with an under eye cream manufacturer

The format changes the base, the pack, the fill cost and the price your customer will accept. These are the five formats Indian brands most often ask for:

  • Under-eye cream: the classic emulsion. It carries oil-soluble actives well and suits dryness and fine lines. Keep it light enough that it does not migrate into the eyes.
  • Under-eye gel: water-based, quick to absorb and pleasant in humid weather. An under eye gel is a natural fit for puffiness and for oily or combination skin.
  • Eye serum: a fluid with a higher share of actives, usually sold in a dropper or airless pump.
  • Roll-on: a gel or serum in a bottle with a metal ball applicator. The cool metal adds a de-puffing feel that shoppers notice on the first use.
  • Instant smoother: a film-forming product that visibly blurs fine lines for a few hours. It sells on visible effect, so describe it as temporary.

For a first range, many brands launch one cream or gel and add a roll-on later. Starting with two formats at once doubles the testing work and the packaging orders without doubling the sales.

Ingredients and delivery systems for the eye area

The skin under the eyes is thinner than on the cheeks and moves every time we blink or smile, so the eye area tends to show irritation quickly. That is why eye products usually use lower active levels, milder preservatives and little or no fragrance compared with face products. Common building blocks include:

  • Caffeine for products positioned around puffiness and tired-looking eyes.
  • Niacinamide for an even, brighter look where pigment is part of the problem.
  • Peptides for products aimed at fine lines and firmness.
  • Retinol, used at carefully set levels for the eye area, ideally in a controlled-release form.
  • Hyaluronic acid and glycerin for hydration, which makes almost every eye product feel better.
  • Plant extracts for brands with a botanical story, chosen with an eye on irritation potential.

How an active is delivered matters as much as how much you add. Liposomes, microsponges and encapsulation can release an ingredient gradually, which helps when you want a retinoid without the usual redness around the eyes. Our guide to liposomal and encapsulated delivery systems explains how these carriers work and what to ask about them.

Safety, stability and testing for eye-area products

Eye products deserve a stricter test plan than an ordinary body lotion. Ask your manufacturer to walk you through each layer below before you approve artwork.

A few points are worth knowing in more detail:

  • Stability in Indian conditions: a gel that looks perfect in an air-conditioned lab can thin out or separate in a hot delivery van. Ask what temperatures the samples were held at and for how long.
  • Pack compatibility: retinol and some plant extracts react with air and light, so the pack is part of the formula. Ask for data in the actual tube or pump you will sell.
  • Animal-free testing: India does not allow cosmetics to be tested on animals, so eye safety is assessed with ingredient data, lab methods and supervised human use tests. One example of a lab method is OECD Test Guideline 492, which uses reconstructed human cornea-like tissue to identify materials that do not need an eye-irritation classification.
  • User testing: if you plan to say "ophthalmologist tested" or "dermatologically tested", the test must be done and documented before the claim goes on the pack.

Claims and labels: what you can and cannot say

Eye products attract bold promises, and that is exactly where brands get into trouble. A cosmetic in India is meant to cleanse, beautify or improve appearance. It is not meant to treat a disease. The Cosmetics Rules, 2020 forbid labels that give the buyer a false or misleading idea about the product. CDSCO's own list of common problems in import applications includes wording that makes a cosmetic look like a drug, such as claims of treating a disease or disorder, "Drug Facts" panels and "Dermatologist recommended" statements.

In practice, that means:

  • Prefer "reduces the look of dark circles" or "helps the under-eye area look brighter" over "removes dark circles".
  • Do not promise permanent results, and avoid words such as cure, heal or treat.
  • Back any numbers in your claims with a documented test.
  • Make sure the label carries the details the rules call for, including the batch number, the manufacturing licence number and the expiry or "use before" date, which CDSCO lists among the items most often missing.

Your manufacturer should check artwork against the formula before printing. It costs far less to correct a proof than to relabel finished stock.

Packaging choices for eye care

The pack decides how hygienic the product stays and how well its actives last. A few rules of thumb:

  • Squeeze tubes with a fine nozzle are affordable, light to ship and let customers take a small amount.
  • Airless pumps protect sensitive actives such as retinol from air and give a measured dose.
  • Roll-on bottles with metal balls suit gels and serums aimed at puffiness.
  • Open jars look premium but expose the formula to air and fingers each time, so they are a weaker choice for oxidisable actives.

Choose the pack early, because every stability and compatibility test should be run in it.

Questions to ask an under eye cream manufacturer

Use this checklist in your first call or factory visit:

  1. Which under-eye formulas do you already have, and what concern was each one built for?
  2. How do you set active levels for the eye area, and can you explain the reasoning?
  3. Which delivery systems can you use for retinol or other sensitive actives?
  4. What stability conditions and duration do you test, and in which pack?
  5. How do you assess eye-area safety without animal testing?
  6. Will you review our claims and artwork against the formula before printing?
  7. What is the minimum batch size, and how does it change by pack? Our MOQ guide explains the factors behind minimum orders.
  8. How long do samples take, and how many revision rounds are included?
  9. Who owns the formula if we develop it together?

Frequently asked questions

Is an under eye cream really different from a face cream?

Yes, in the way it is designed. Eye products usually use lighter textures, lower active levels, gentler preservatives and little or no fragrance, because the skin around the eyes is thinner and reacts faster. They are also tested more carefully for eye-area comfort.

How long does it take to develop an under eye cream?

At Ecosmetix, lab samples usually take about 14 days from a clear brief. The full timeline then depends on revision rounds, stability and safety testing, claim tests and packaging lead times.

What is the minimum order quantity for under eye cream manufacturing?

It depends on the formula, the pack and whether you choose a ready formula or a custom one. Small eye packs mean more units per batch, so ask your manufacturer for the minimum in units and in kilograms for your chosen pack.

Can retinol be used in an under eye cream?

It can, at levels set for the eye area and preferably in a controlled-release form such as a microsponge. A protective pack such as an airless pump and clear usage directions help keep the product gentle and stable.

Can I claim that my cream removes dark circles?

Avoid it. A cosmetic should describe how the skin looks, for example that it reduces the look of dark circles, and any specific result should be supported by a documented test. Words that suggest treating a condition make a cosmetic look like a drug.

Which packaging is best for an under eye product?

Fine-nozzle tubes are practical and affordable, airless pumps protect sensitive actives, and roll-ons with metal balls suit gels for puffiness. Open jars are the weakest choice for actives that react with air.

Is ophthalmologist testing compulsory for eye creams in India?

It is not a general label requirement, but if you print a claim such as ophthalmologist tested, you need a documented test behind it. Many brands run supervised user tests for eye products because customers expect them.
