Face Wash Manufacturer in India: Formats, Surfactants, Process and Questions to Ask

21 November 2026 · By the Ecosmetix R&D team

A face wash is usually the first SKU a new skin care brand puts on shelf, and it is the one customers finish fastest and buy again. That makes the choice of face wash manufacturer a quiet driver of your repeat sales: if the cleanser leaves skin tight, foams weakly in hard water or separates in the tube by summer, shoppers rarely give the rest of your range a chance.

This guide is for founders planning a cleanser for the Indian market. It covers the formats you can choose from, how the surfactant system decides mildness, which actives make sense in a rinse-off product, the stages of a face wash project, the label rules that apply and the questions to put to any factory. We make cleansers for brands at our plant in Ambala, Haryana, so we also explain how we handle these projects. Sources are listed at the end.

What a face wash manufacturer handles for your brand

A licensed manufacturer can do almost everything between your idea and a packed carton. How much you hand over depends on the route you pick. With private label, you start from a cleanser that is already developed and tested, then change fragrance, colour, a hero active and the pack. With custom formulation, our R&D team builds the formula around your brief. With pure third-party manufacturing, where we act as a third party face wash manufacturer, you bring a formula and we make, fill and pack it under our licence.

  • The manufacturer: formula development or adaptation, lab samples, stability checks in the chosen pack, batch manufacturing, quality control, filling, coding and dispatch.
  • Your brand: target customer, price point, brand name, artwork, marketing and sales channels.
  • Decided together: the claim list and the pack, since both must match what the formula and its test data can support.

If you are still deciding between these routes, our comparison of private label, contract manufacturing and custom formulation goes through the trade-offs.

Surfactants decide how mild a face wash feels

Every cleanser removes oil and dirt with surfactants: molecules with one end that holds onto oil and one that holds onto water, so grime can be rinsed away. The same action can also remove some of the skin's own lipids and disturb the outer layer of the skin, the stratum corneum. Consumer guidance from the American Academy of Dermatology likewise recommends a gentle, non-abrasive cleanser for the face. Reviews of cleanser science describe this barrier damage and the technologies used to reduce it, which is why mildness is a formulation choice and not just a marketing word.

A review in Dermatologic Therapy on managing patient skin puts it simply: "Care must be taken to minimize any further weakening of the SC barrier during cleansing." The same review points to cleansers based on mild synthetic surfactants and emollients for people with acne, rosacea, eczema or skin recovering from peels.

In practice a face wash manufacturer chooses between a few families of surfactant:

  • Sulphate-based systems: strong, cheap foam. Usually too harsh as the main cleanser for a daily face product unless heavily buffered with milder co-surfactants.
  • Amino-acid surfactants (for example glycinates and glutamates): soft, creamy lather and a gentle after-feel. A common base for sulphate-free face washes.
  • Glucosides: sugar-derived, very mild, often paired with amino-acid surfactants to build foam.
  • Betaines and other amphoterics: used alongside the main surfactant to improve mildness and foam stability.

At Ecosmetix we build sulphate-free face washes on amino-acid and glucoside surfactant systems. If a brief asks for a big lather on a small budget, we explain what each route does to feel and cost before the first sample is made, so the trade-off is your decision and not a surprise.

Why face wash pH matters

Healthy skin is slightly acidic. A study of skin surface pH published in the International Journal of Cosmetic Science reported that natural skin surface pH is on average below 5, and that this acidic state suits the skin's resident flora. Traditional soap is alkaline, so a well-built synthetic detergent (syndet) cleanser has a clear advantage: it can be set much closer to skin pH.

Ask any manufacturer what pH range the face wash is designed for, and whether pH is checked on every batch at release. A specification without a pH limit is a warning sign.

Actives that work in a rinse-off face wash

A face wash stays on skin for well under a minute, so it cannot do the job of a serum. Actives in a cleanser are best chosen for what they can do in that short contact time and for the story they tell next to the rest of the range. Ingredients we commonly work with in this category include:

  • Salicylic acid: oil-soluble, the usual choice for oily and acne-prone ranges.
  • Glycolic and lactic acid: surface exfoliation for dull or uneven-looking skin; pH and level need care to keep the product comfortable.
  • Niacinamide: popular for oil-control and "glow" positioning, and easy to explain on a label.
  • Ceramides: fit gentle, barrier-care cleansers for dry or sensitive skin.
  • Vitamin C: supports a brightening range story; stability has to be confirmed in the final pack.
  • Red and green clay: oil absorption and a visible, tactile difference for deep-cleansing products.

Each active has permitted levels and labelling conditions, and those depend on the product type and the claim. Your manufacturer should confirm the regulatory position for every active and level before you approve a sample. For acne-focused ranges, our guide to grading an acne skin care line shows how a cleanser fits alongside treatment products, and our anti-acne range includes gel and foam cleansers matched to treatment steps.

How face wash manufacturing works, stage by stage

A cleanser project has fewer test steps than a sunscreen, but the order still matters. Skipping stability in the real pack is the most common reason a face wash looks fine in the lab and fails in a warehouse.

  1. Brief. Tell us the skin type, format, foam and feel you want, hero actives, any free-from claims, fragrance direction, pack size and target cost. A benchmark product helps more than adjectives. Our guide to writing a cosmetic product brief lists what to include.
  2. Concept. R&D proposes a surfactant route and active system, and flags anything in the brief that conflicts, such as very high foam with very low irritation at a low cost.
  3. Lab sample. Lab samples are usually ready within 14 days. Use them the way your customers will: on wet skin, in your own tap water, morning and night.
  4. Approve. You give feedback, we fine-tune, and the formula is locked. Stability checks and any claim testing run on the locked version. Across our ranges, 99% of formulas are approved at first selection.
  5. Pack. Tube, pump or foaming pump is confirmed, the formula is checked for compatibility with it, and artwork goes through label checks.
  6. Produce. The batch is made, tested against its specification, filled, coded and dispatched. Our plant has a mixing capacity of 16,000 kg a day and filling capacity of 60,000 tubes a day, so a reorder does not have to wait behind a long queue.

What to test before launch

  • Stability in the final pack at raised temperature and room temperature, watching viscosity, colour, odour, pH and separation. Gels thin and foams can separate when Indian summers hit a warehouse.
  • Preservative performance, because a water-rich cleanser opened in a humid bathroom is a hard environment.
  • Foam and rinse behaviour in hard and soft water, since water quality varies a lot across Indian cities.
  • Claim tests only for the claims you will print, such as "dermatologically tested" or "suitable for sensitive skin".

Rules that apply to a face wash label in India

A face wash sold in India is a cosmetic. It has to be made at a licensed facility under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020, published by CDSCO, and the label must meet those rules. Brand owners who do not have their own plant should read our explainer on the cosmetic loan licence and brand owners.

The Legal Metrology (Packaged Commodities) Rules, 2011 also apply to retail packs. Rule 6 lists the declarations to be made on every package, including the name and address of the manufacturer and packer, the common or generic name of the product, the net quantity, the month and year of manufacture or packing, the retail sale price, and consumer care contact details. For cosmetics, the rules point to the cosmetic regulations for some of these details, so your manufacturer's regulatory team should sign off the final artwork.

Keep claims honest. "Sulphate-free", "pH balanced" or "for acne-prone skin" are fine when the formula and data support them. Medical claims such as "cures acne" turn a cosmetic into something else entirely and should not appear on a face wash.

Questions to ask a face wash manufacturer

Use these in your first call. They work for any factory, including ours, and the general checklist in our article on choosing a third-party cosmetic manufacturer in India covers the wider points.

  • Which surfactant system do you propose for my skin type, and why?
  • What pH range is the formula designed for, and is it tested on every batch?
  • Do you have ready face wash formulas I can start from, and what can I change in them?
  • How long do lab samples take, and how many revision rounds are included?
  • What stability testing will you run, and in which pack?
  • Which claims can this formula support, and what tests would each one need?
  • What is the MOQ for my pack size, and does it change for a ready formula versus a custom one?
  • What is your daily mixing and filling capacity for reorders?
  • Who checks my label against the Cosmetics Rules and Legal Metrology requirements?

Ask for answers on MOQ, costing and timelines in writing, per SKU and per pack size. MOQ depends on the product, the pack and how much is customised, so a single number quoted without your brief is not a real answer.

Frequently asked questions

How do I start my own face wash brand in India?

Decide your target skin type and price, pick a format and a manufacturing route (private label, custom formulation or third-party manufacturing), brief a licensed manufacturer, approve samples, run stability in your final pack, finish label checks and then place your first production order.

What is the difference between a private label and a custom face wash?

A private label face wash starts from a formula the manufacturer has already developed and tested; you change details such as fragrance, a hero active and the pack. A custom face wash is built from your brief, which takes longer but gives you a formula that is distinct to your brand.

Can a manufacturer make a sulphate-free face wash?

Yes. Sulphate-free cleansers are usually built on amino-acid and glucoside surfactants with amphoteric co-surfactants. At Ecosmetix these mild systems are the standard base for sulphate-free face washes.

Which face wash format is best for oily skin?

Gel and foaming face washes are the usual choices for oily and combination skin, and a salicylic acid cleanser suits acne-prone buyers. Clay cleansers work as a weekly deep-cleansing step.

Why is pH important in a face wash?

Skin's surface is naturally acidic, on average below pH 5. A cleanser formulated close to skin pH is generally kinder than an alkaline soap, so ask your manufacturer for the pH range and whether it is checked on every batch.

How long does it take to make a face wash for my brand?

At Ecosmetix lab samples are usually ready within 14 days. Feedback rounds, stability checks in the final pack, artwork and production add to the overall time, so plan your launch date around those stages.

What is the minimum order quantity for a face wash?

MOQ depends on the product, the pack size and how much is customised. Share your SKU sizes and launch volume in a brief and the manufacturer should confirm MOQ and costing in writing.

Do I need a licence to sell a face wash under my own brand?

The face wash must be made at a facility licensed under the Cosmetics Rules, 2020. Brand owners without their own plant work with a licensed manufacturer; our article on the cosmetic loan licence explains the options.
